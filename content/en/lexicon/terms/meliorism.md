Pragmatist conviction that human effort might improve conditions; neither optimism (improvement guaranteed) nor pessimism (improvement impossible) but the belief that intelligent action *might* make a difference. Dewey's driving faith, constitutive of pragmatism itself. Author argues meliorism "spins in a void" when capital has withdrawn the substrate for improvement: Eden is not a problem awaiting solution but a condition to inhabit. Meliorism presupposes that "better" and "worse" are measurable on a stable scale; *lift* is precisely what has deactivated that scale. What survives: fallibilism without meliorism—experimentation without the expectation of improvement.
