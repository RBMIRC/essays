Lütticken's concept of re-staging past gestures under altered conditions; not faithful reproduction but revelation of what the original contained without knowing it. Reenactment differs from repetition: it acknowledges the gap between past and present while using that gap productively. De Beyter's limousine (dragged across junkyard earth, wheels locked against trajectory) and Gaignard's still (distilling rosemary through automobile exhaust pipes) are reenactments of industrial endings—they restage, in workshop present, an ending that occurred in an undatable past.

**References:**
- LÜTTICKEN, Sven, ed. *Life, Once More: Forms of Reenactment in Contemporary Art*. Rotterdam: Witte de With, 2005.
- SCHNEIDER, Rebecca. *Performing Remains: Art and War in Times of Theatrical Reenactment*. London: Routledge, 2011.
