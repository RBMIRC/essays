American philosophical tradition (Peirce, James, Dewey) emphasizing experience, inquiry, and consequences over abstract truth. Core commitments: fallibilism (all knowledge provisional), social character of inquiry (knowledge requires interlocutors), experimental method (hypotheses tested through action). BMC's theoretical foundation—Rice had read Dewey's *Democracy and Education* before founding the college; Dewey himself stayed at BMC in 1934–35. Author argues pragmatism's meliorist assumptions require critique under exocapitalist conditions: the framework survives but must be "rearmed"—fallibilism without progress, inquiry without consensus, aesthetic experience without resolution.
