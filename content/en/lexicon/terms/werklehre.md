Albers's pedagogical method at BMC; learning through direct engagement with materials, approaching them with questions rather than plans. Students worked with paper, wire, wood—not to produce finished objects but to discover material properties through experimentation. Embodied Deweyan fallibilism in artistic practice: the material teaches the student, not the reverse. Albers was not Deweyan in a doctrinal sense but shared the conviction that learning proceeds through doing and that perception is educated through direct experience of materials. The Eden project's workshops inherit this principle: materials (territory, archive, residents) are approached as problems to engage rather than resources to exploit.
