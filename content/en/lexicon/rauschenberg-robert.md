American artist (1925-2008), studied at Black Mountain College (1948-1949, 1951-1952) under Albers and with Cage. *White Paintings* (1951) anticipated Cage's *4'33"*—surfaces registering ambient light and shadow. Participated in *Theatre Piece No. 1* (1952). Developed "Combines" (1954-1964) integrating painting with found objects, collapsing distinction between art and non-art materials. Rauschenberg's BMC formation shows how encounter between disciplines (Albers's material rigor, Cage's openness to chance) generates hybrid practices. His dictum: "painting relates to both art and life... I try to act in the gap between the two."
