Dewey's concept (*Art as Experience*, 1934) of organism in active transaction with environment. Biological grounding of aesthetic experience through rhythm of doing and undergoing. "No creature lives merely under its skin; its subcutaneous organs are means of connection with what lies beyond its bodily frame." The live creature is a *body*—carnal organism in transaction with physical and social environment. This insistence on corporeality makes Dewey an unexpected resource under exocapitalist conditions where capital operates without bodies. The Deweyan categories that survive critique are all categories of the body: doing, co-presence, transaction with environment, sensory attention.

**References:**
- DEWEY, John. *Art as Experience*. New York: Minton, Balch & Company, 1934.
- PUTNAM, Ruth Anna. "Reflections on the Future of Pragmatism." In Hilary and Ruth Anna Putnam, *Pragmatism as a Way of Life*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 2017.
