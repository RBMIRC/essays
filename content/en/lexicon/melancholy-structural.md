*Author's term* drawing on Freud's distinction between mourning and melancholy. Mourning accepts loss and frees itself from the lost object; melancholy maintains the lost object as spectral presence, neither living nor dead. Eden is sustained by structural melancholy: the industrial past, the modernist promise, BMC's pedagogical utopia—these endings structure the present without being datable to a single moment. The separation is irreversible but the lost object continues to structure what follows. Every artistic gesture at Eden reenacts this separation. Not an obstacle to overcome but the condition in which the work operates.
