Jack Burnham's concept (1968) for art that emphasizes relationships, processes, and systems over discrete objects. In "Systems Esthetics" (*Artforum*), Burnham argued that the art object was giving way to the art system: focus shifts from what is made to how things interact. Anticipated network art, relational aesthetics, and computational practices. Systems aesthetics treats art as information processing rather than object production—relevant to Author's interest in proto-computational practices at BMC.

**References:**
- BURNHAM, Jack. "Systems Esthetics." *Artforum* 7, no. 1 (1968): 30-35.
