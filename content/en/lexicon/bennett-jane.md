American political theorist. *Vibrant Matter: A Political Ecology of Things* (2010) develops "vital materialism"—attending to the agency of nonhuman things (metals, worms, electricity). Against viewing matter as passive stuff awaiting human forming; for recognizing "thing-power" that exceeds human intention. Draws on Spinoza, Deleuze, and Latour to theorize distributed agency across human-nonhuman assemblages. Relevant to understanding how materials participate in workshop practices.

**Key Concepts:** Vibrant Matter, Thing-Power, Vital Materialism
