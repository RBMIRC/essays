*Author's term* for archival method that locates significance in what is missing rather than what is present. Where traditional archival science organizes existing materials, void detection maps lacunae: what was destroyed, never collected, systematically excluded, or accidentally lost. The void becomes generative: absence as positive condition rather than mere lack. Computational methods enable new forms of void detection—algorithms trained on existing archives can reveal what patterns they cannot produce.
